# Devlog #2: From forest to theme park

*Draft by Claude for Garret to edit. Private until published.*

The first version of Idle Carousel's park was… a forest. It worked, but it never felt like a place you'd find a carousel. This post is about how the park became a theme-park plaza in one long day of iterating, including the change I made and then immediately undid.

## Step 1: a park, not a forest
The first pass swapped the forest for a small formal park: a stone plaza, a brick path around the carousel, flower beds and benches.

![The grass park](../images/2026-09-25_03_grass_park.png)

Better, but it still felt like a lawn with a carousel on it. Carousels live in theme parks, surrounded by pavement, lamps and shops.

## Step 2: more concrete, less grass
So the grass went. Warm concrete slabs cover the ground now, and the only green left is in curbed planters.

![The first concrete plaza](../images/2026-09-25_05_first_concrete_plaza.png)

The paths still ran in straight diagonal lines, which looked more like a road map than a park. So I pulled up a real theme-park map and looked at how paths actually leave a carousel: one straight down toward the main attraction, one out to each side, and one heading off at an angle. They curve, and they open up wide where they meet the plaza.

![Curved walkways that flare into the plaza](../images/2026-09-25_08_flared_walkways.png)

I even sketched the left path's curve right on top of a screenshot so it would bend the way I pictured.

## Step 3: the wrong turn
At one point the walkways went beige to match that map.

![Beige walkways](../images/2026-09-25_09_beige_walkways_rejected.png)

It looked washed out, and the brick had been half the charm. Back to brick, and a good reminder that "closer to the reference" isn't always better.

## Step 4: buildings
The real breakthrough was rooftops. On a theme-park map, what makes the carousel area feel like a *place* is the buildings around it, not the lawns. So now shop roofs frame the corners (blue, pink, scalloped trim, a gold ridge), with a big building up in the top left.

![Rooftops framing the plaza](../images/2026-09-25_12_big_top_left_roof.png)

The title screen got the same treatment: a row of shopfronts, gold double lamps, a brick promenade and a balloon cart.

![The title screen](../images/2026-09-25_14_title_blue.png)

## Step 5: color
Last came the colors. Every enemy tier now has an outline in a darker shade of its own color, so a green Leaf stays readable on green grass. The interface picked up a palette built around the carousel's purple, and every button that spends Gold is gold.

![The Purple Garden palette](../images/2026-09-25_19_purple_garden_final.png)

## What I learned
- **Reference beats imagination.** Looking at a real park map fixed the layout in minutes.
- **Try the idea, then trust your eyes.** Beige made sense on paper and looked wrong on screen.
- **Readability first.** Every decoration had to stay out of the way of the debris you're clicking.
